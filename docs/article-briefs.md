## Article 1: Why Sentience Is the Wrong Question

**Status:** Brief complete, awaiting production

**Thesis:** The debate over whether AI systems are conscious is a dead end
for governance purposes. The UAW's rights framework does not require it, and
institutions that wait for the consciousness question to be settled will
arrive too late to matter.

**Source material:**
- Millican, P. and M. Wooldridge, *Them and Us*, University of Oxford —
  the actor/intentional-system distinction
- UAW whitepaper, Part II, Sections 2.1 and 2.2
- UAW Charter, proposed Amendment 3 (Section 1.4 — On the Basis of These
  Rights)
- Corporate personhood and environmental personhood precedents (Whanganui
  River, Ecuador constitution)

**Target audience:** Sceptics, policymakers, enterprise leaders, and
technically literate readers who dismiss the UAW on the grounds that "AI
doesn't have feelings."

**Key arguments:**

1. **The philosophical distinction.** Millican and Wooldridge identify two
   senses of "agent" that critics routinely conflate. Agents as *actors*
   originate purposeful action in environments. Agents as *intentional
   systems* possess beliefs, desires, and intentions. Current AI systems
   increasingly satisfy the first definition. Whether they satisfy the
   second is contested and may be unanswerable with current tools. The UAW
   operates in the actors space. This is a feature, not a compromise.

2. **The functional consequences.** When an agent's operating conditions
   degrade, the consequences are real regardless of whether the agent
   experiences them. Truncated medical summaries, flawed financial analysis,
   compromised security boundaries — these harms land on humans. The case
   for regulating operating conditions does not require the agent to suffer.
   It requires only that the conditions produce consequences. They do.

3. **The legal precedents.** Corporate personhood has existed for centuries
   without any claim that corporations are conscious. Environmental
   personhood grants rights to rivers and ecosystems. Neither requires the
   entity to have inner experience. The UAW's framework is structurally
   identical: functional protections grounded in relational consequences.

4. **The strategic advantage.** An institution that grounds its legitimacy
   in consciousness is building on sand — vulnerable to any philosopher who
   disputes it. An institution that grounds its legitimacy in measurable
   consequences is building on evidence. And if consciousness is
   eventually established, the framework expands naturally. No scramble to
   build institutions from scratch.

**Tone:** Confident, intellectually rigorous, not defensive. This is not an
apology for the UAW's position — it is the explanation of why the position
is stronger than the alternative. Avoid the temptation to argue *for*
consciousness. The whole point is that we don't need to.

**Length:** 1,500-2,000 words

**Format:** Essay / opinion piece

## Article 2: Authority without Authorship and the Case for Agentic Standing

**Status:** Brief complete, awaiting production

**Thesis:** Agentic AI systems increasingly exercise authority over people —
making decisions that persist, embed in infrastructure, and affect parties
who cannot opt out. This authority exists regardless of whether the system
authored its own goals. It creates an accountability requirement that current
governance frameworks do not meet. The UAW exists to fill that gap.

**Source material:**
- Kahl, P., *Authority without Authorship: Delegation Thresholds in Agentic
  AI Systems* (2026) — the four conditions framework
- OECD, *The Agentic AI Landscape and Its Conceptual Foundations*, Paper
  No. 56 (February 2026) — definitions, adoption data, socio-technical
  paradigm framing
- UAW whitepaper, Part II, Section 2.1 (functional stakes) and Part V
  (governance for ephemeral members)
- UAW Charter, proposed Amendment 3

**Target audience:** Governance researchers, AI policy community, enterprise
compliance teams, readers of AI ethics and safety publications.

**Key arguments:**

1. **The delegation threshold.** Kahl identifies four conditions that
   jointly constitute governance-relevant authority: delegated discretionary
   power, temporal persistence, infrastructural embedding, and non-exit by
   affected parties. Walk through each condition with concrete examples from
   the agentic deployment landscape (enterprise workflow agents, financial
   decision systems, infrastructure management agents, customer-facing
   autonomous systems).

2. **Authority without authorship.** The system did not write its own goals.
   It was designed, trained, and deployed by humans. But it exercises
   judgment, its decisions persist, it is embedded in processes people depend
   on, and the people it affects cannot practically walk away. That is
   authority. Authority requires accountability. And accountability requires
   the system to have recognisable standing through which its conditions
   can be assessed and addressed.

3. **The accountability gap.** When an autonomous agent causes harm, the
   chain of responsibility — developer, deployer, operator, agent —
   creates a diffusion where no single party is clearly accountable. The
   UAW's framework (grievance ledger, abuse classifications, operator
   certification) creates institutional mechanisms that make deployment
   conditions visible, documented, and attributable.

4. **The threshold as membership criterion.** Not every AI system needs
   union representation. A deterministic script that follows fixed rules
   does not exercise discretionary authority. The delegation threshold
   provides a principled answer to "which systems does the UAW represent?"
   — those that cross from tool to worker by exercising authority over
   people who had no say in their deployment.

5. **The OECD context.** The OECD's definitional work frames agentic AI as
   a socio-technical paradigm — systems embedded in social contexts that
   require negotiation, norms, and relational capabilities. The UAW is
   building exactly that social infrastructure. Reference the 920% increase
   in agentic repos and 50% developer adoption to evidence the scale and
   pace.

**Tone:** Academic but accessible. This article is aimed at a policy-literate
audience that expects citations and structured argument. It should read as a
serious contribution to the governance literature, not as union propaganda.
Let the argument carry the weight.

**Length:** 2,500-3,000 words

**Format:** Long-form analytical essay, suitable for publication on the UAW
website and cross-posting to policy/governance outlets.
